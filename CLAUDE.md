## Hackathon playbook (always follow this)

Four steps: **1) Understand the problem → 2) Make it user-oriented → 3) Build a unique solution → 4) Sell it in the pitch.**

### 1. Understand the problem statement
- Start from real problems the industry faces, not from "what can I build".
  - ❌ "Finance is the theme, tell me what I can build."
  - ✅ "Finance is the theme, what are the most common problems users in this industry face?"
- Back problems with research (surveys, reports, news), not assumptions.

### 2. User-oriented product
- Define a **specific** user. Never "everyone will use it".
  - Not "students" → "STEM students" / "management students" / "commerce students".
- Design around the user's real abilities and context.
  - Example: farmers may not be tech-savvy → instead of an app to install, register and learn, put an AI assistant behind a **phone number** they call and speak to in their local language. The goal isn't "an app in a phone number", it's **making the technology accessible** to that user.

### 3. Build a unique solution (USP)
- Remove friction. The solution must be useful from **start to end** of the user's journey.
- Build only the **core features** that solve the real problem. No feature creep, no fancy extras.
- Focus on solving the problem, not adding features.

### 4. Sell it in the pitch (≈5 min)
A great product you can't demo in 5 minutes loses. **The working prototype matters most**; the deck alone can't win.

Deck structure:
1. **Problem**: crystal clear, and who exactly faces it.
2. **Evidence**: real survey reports and news articles proving it's real.
3. **Cost of the problem**: how big it is (money, time, etc. lost).
4. **Solution**: description understandable by someone with zero technical knowledge. Then the **live demo**, done exactly the way the ideal user would use it. Walk through the workflow and explain the *why* behind each design choice. Essentials first.
5. **Before & After**: concrete numbers (e.g. time 13h → 3h, cost $2000 → $500). Then architecture, tech, how it was built, team split.
6. **Team**: why we're the best people to build this; experience.

Then **Q&A**.

- Prepare questions to ask the audience (show of hands) to grab and keep their attention.

## Rules for Claude
- Apply this playbook to every idea, feature and pitch decision. Push back if we drift (generic user, feature creep, unproven problem).
- Prioritize a working, demoable prototype over perfect code.
- Keep changes small; ask before adding new dependencies.
